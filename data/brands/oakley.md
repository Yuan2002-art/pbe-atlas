A Southern California eyewear and equipment brand whose identity was built on
patents, testing and a hard-edged industrial aesthetic. Because eyewear is worn
visibly in competition, Oakley's credibility has historically been argued
through athletes on start lines.

Relevant here because a small worn object forces the brand to build **spatial**
experience around something almost invisible at scale.

**Why this is still unsourced.** Oakley publishes a heritage page at
`oakley.com/en-us/lp/heritage`, and it is the right page — its title is
"Unveiling Oakley Heritage in Eyewear Innovation" and the heading "HOW WE
CHANGED THE EYEWEAR GAME" is present in the markup. But its body never loads for
this client: the document returns only shop navigation and a Luxottica cookie
notice, the content stays behind that wall after scrolling, and the page would
not render for input so the consent banner could not be dismissed. Nothing
Oakley says about itself could be quoted.

What this record claims and still needs a source for: the 1975 founding, the
country, and the Southern California origin. A search summary attributes 1975 to
Jim Jannard leaving a sales job and naming the company after his English Setter,
Oakley Anne — **not recorded here, because no retrieved page states it.**

**Where to look next:** the heritage page opened in a normal browser, with the
cookie banner dismissed, and cited from there. Luxottica, Oakley's owner, also
publishes brand history in investor material, which is usually retrievable.

An eyewear and equipment brand whose identity was built on patents, testing and
a hard-edged industrial aesthetic, and which is now a proprietary brand of
EssilorLuxottica.

**It did not begin as an eyewear company.** Oakley's own heritage page is blunt
about it — "Before eyewear came into view, a motorcycle grip blazed the trail."
The first product was the Oakley Grip, a motorcycle handlebar grip in a
proprietary rubber compound the brand registered as *Unobtainium*. Goggles
followed in 1980, and the page describes that move as "what was meant as a
marketing stunt… simply by trying to gain more exposure in the grip industry."
Sunglasses came only in 1984, with the Eyeshade. The sequence matters for this
Atlas: the optics came out of a parts business, which is why the brand argues in
the language of materials and testing rather than of fashion.

**Two things in the brand's own account bear directly on the thesis.**

The first is that Oakley states outright that it used *the athlete's body as its
media buy*. Of the 1980 goggle: "Our approach to making the logo highly visible
on the lens front and strap set the stage for our entrance into the optics
arena. We were getting exposure from the most respected people in the motocross
community before our budget could stretch to making endorsement deals happen."
That is a brand describing visible wear in competition as a deliberate substitute
for paid endorsement — and it is the reason a company selling a small worn object
has to build spatial experience around something almost invisible at scale.

The second is the **Factory Pilot Team**, which the page calls "a first-of-its-kind
community." It describes an R&D department staffed by competing BMX riders —
"The doers and makers bringing the products to life during the week were the same
people racing in Oakley products on the weekend" — and customers who "weren't just
buying into the products, they were giving their two cents on the latest prototype
too." Community-as-development, stated by the brand, in the 1980s. Whether any
current Oakley activation still runs on that mechanism is a question for a case,
not for this record.

**What is sourced:** 1975, Jim Jannard, the dog, the grip-before-eyewear
sequence, the 1980 and 1984 product dates, and the two passages above — all from
Oakley's heritage page. The EssilorLuxottica listing sources the current
ownership.

**What is not: `country` is empty, deliberately.** Oakley is universally
described as American and as Southern Californian, and the earlier version of
this record asserted both. Neither is stated on any page retrieved. The heritage
page's nearest approach is a scene set on the Pacific Coast Highway in 1984, and
the site footer reads "Copyright ©2024 Oakley, Inc." — a US corporate suffix and
a Californian road, but not a claim about where the company was founded or is
based. Oakley's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy carry no corporate address, and
EssilorLuxottica's brands section has no Oakley page of its own: it links
straight out to oakley.com. Following the same rule applied to Compressport, the
field stays blank rather than inferring a headquarters.

`positioning` is a reading, not a quotation.

**Where to look next for the country:** an EssilorLuxottica annual report or
20-F, which lists subsidiaries with their jurisdictions, or a US trademark
registration for the Oakley word mark, which carries the registrant's address.

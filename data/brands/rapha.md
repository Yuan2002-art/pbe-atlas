Rapha built its brand on editorial storytelling about endurance and on the
Clubhouse — a physical format combining café, shop, ride start and viewing room
that has since been widely imitated across endurance sport.

Relevant here because the Clubhouse is the most explicit case of retail designed
primarily as **community infrastructure**.

**What is verified:** 2004, in Rapha's own words. The same sentence also
supports the positioning line more directly than most records here manage —
Rapha itself frames cycling as "the toughest and most beautiful sport in the
world" and describes its work as "documenting its transformational power". The
"suffering-centred culture" reading is close to the brand's own framing rather
than imposed on it.

**What is not:** the United Kingdom. This page states the year and not the
place. Rapha's separate about page gives London as the founding city and Simon
Mottram as founder, but it was not retrieved, so `country` is unconfirmed here
even though the `.cc/gb/` domain and the brand's history make it uncontroversial.
The Clubhouse format, and the claim that it has been widely imitated, are the
author's reading — and the Clubhouse is the thing this Atlas would actually want
a case on.

A Paris brand that reframed running away from timing and improvement toward
music-adjacent subculture, distressed materials and a deliberately anti-corporate
tone. Small production runs and self-operated spaces keep the audience narrow on
purpose.

Relevant here because its performance claim is almost entirely **cultural** —
useful for testing whether the Atlas's classification can separate cultural
archive from performance archive.

**What is verified:** the 2015 launch, and that the brand frames its own product
around "the High" rather than around times or improvement — both in SATISFY's
own words on its manifesto page. That second point matters, because the
interpretation in this Atlas leans on it: the brand's own copy, not the author,
is what puts the emphasis away from race times.

**What is not:** the country. This record says France and the body says Paris,
and the retrieved page states neither — the brand's founder interview does give
a Paris creative studio, but that page returns 404 to this client, so it cannot
be cited. The subculture reading, the distressed materials and the deliberate
narrowness of the audience are all the author's characterisation.

## Description
A four-day instructional gathering on the mountains above the brand's home city,
combining guided climbs and trail runs, evening technical talks, and a temporary
basecamp of tents for registration, gear loan and repair.

## Strategic purpose
Teach the use of the product rather than sell it, on the assumption that
competence creates a more durable customer than persuasion.

## Archive use
Central example for the argument that instruction is an underrated retail format:
the brand's credibility is transferred through the participant's own body.

## Why this location?
The North Shore is the terrain the brand was founded on, so the claim is
site-specific and cannot be replicated by a competitor without looking borrowed.

## Why this timing?
Late season, after summer crowds and before winter closes the alpine routes,
when conditions are demanding enough to be instructive but not dangerous enough
to exclude novices.

## Why this event?
The academy is the brand's own event, which lets it control the ratio of
instruction to commerce — a ratio it could not control inside a third-party race.

## Why this product?
Loan fleets rather than sales: the products most likely to be misunderstood
(harnesses, shells, technical footwear) are exactly those that benefit from
supervised first use.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
That the brand holds transferable expertise, not only manufacturing capability —
it knows how the mountain is actually used.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Apprenticeship. Participants leave with skills and with a social tie to other
participants, which positions the brand as an entry point into a community rather
than a supplier to it.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Total. There is no retail object to fall back on; the entire case exists as
embodied experience, which makes it the hardest type in this Atlas to document
photographically.

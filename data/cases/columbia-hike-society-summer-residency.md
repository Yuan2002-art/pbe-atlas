## Description
From **23 to 29 August 2026**, Columbia ran an ephemeral chalet in Chamonix
called the **Columbia Hike Society Summer Residency** — staged during UTMB race
week and explicitly against its grain. The announcement describes it as *"une
parenthèse de quiétude et de partage, loin de la quête de performance"*: a pause
for quiet and sharing, far from the pursuit of performance, and modelled on
artist residencies.

The chalet was the base. Walkers gathered there before setting out, came back
for coffee, took part in workshops, or simply sat. One hike or workshop ran each
day, each with a named expert:

- **Sun 23 Aug** — glacier hike to the Mer de Glace, on the geology and minerals
  of the massif.
- **Mon 24 Aug** — mountain nutrition workshop with Justine Villard.
- **Tue 25 Aug** — hiking and yoga in the open with Rose Labeille.
- **Wed 26 Aug** — recovery and post-effort mobility with the physiotherapist
  Aurélie Castillo.
- **Thu 27 Aug** — navigation and trip preparation, with the Whympr app.
- **Fri 28 Aug** — sunset hike with a live podcast recording with the alpinist
  Vadim Druelle, 14:30–22:30.
- **Sat 29 Aug** — a sociable walk out to cheer the UTMB runners.

Sessions named in the coverage include **Frame of Mind**, **Breathe In** and the
daily **Hike Sessions**. They were free and separately booked.

**It happened.** Trade coverage published on 2 September 2026, after the week
closed, reports it in the past tense: the residency "welcomed hikers, hike
leaders, ambassadors of the Columbia Hike Society, and outdoor enthusiasts to a
mountain chalet", and "the chalet became a welcoming hub for conversation and
shared experiences". Columbia's Marketing Manager France, Claire Neuville, is
quoted on the intent: *"we wanted to create a space where people could
experience the mountains differently."* Two Hike Leaders, Rose and Mathilde, are
quoted on the experience.

**Nothing was sold and nothing was put out to test.** No product, no capsule, no
demo fleet and no shop appears in any source.

## Verification notes
**This case has something the others at this edition do not: post-event
coverage.** Most records here rest on announcements. Outdoor Insight's piece is
dated 2 September 2026, after the residency closed, and is written throughout in
the past tense, which is what moves this from "announced" to "happened". It is
trade press rather than independent consumer journalism and reads as though
built on a Columbia release — but unlike the Arc'teryx × Distance source it is
not labelled sponsored, so it is filed as `editorial`.

**The address was never published, and I could not find one.** Four sources were
retrieved — the two trade pieces, the brand's own Eventbrite listing and the
event organiser — and none gives a street address. The Eventbrite listing, which
is Columbia's own channel and would normally carry a venue, gives only
"Chamonix-Mont-Blanc, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes". A third-party listing describes the
exact meeting point as being sent to participants by WhatsApp beforehand, which
is consistent with there never having been a public address. The pin is
therefore the town centre.

A search summary did offer "89 Chemin de la Para" as the chalet address. **It is
not recorded here, because no retrieved page states it.** A number that appears
only in a search engine's summary of pages that do not contain it is exactly the
kind of fact this Atlas must not absorb.

**The dates precede the edition's own window.** The residency ran 23–29 August;
UTMB Mont-Blanc 2026 is recorded as 24–30 August. So the first day fell before
race week opened and the last day before it closed. That is not an error in
either record — it is what the sources say — but it means the occupancy bar on
the event page starts outside the edition.

**Whether Columbia had UTMB's sanction is unknown.** The announcement places the
residency "en marge de l'UTMB" — alongside or at the edge of it — and no source
describes a partnership, a licence or an official status. It is recorded against
the 2026 edition because that is the week it occupied, not because a
relationship is established.

**The session count is unconfirmed.** A search summary gives eleven sessions;
the retrieved day-by-day programme lists seven days with one principal
experience each, and the trade coverage names three formats (Frame of Mind,
Breathe In, Hike Sessions) without a total. Seven days are documented; eleven is
not, and is not claimed above.

**No spatial information is recorded anywhere.** Not the chalet's size, layout,
rooms, furniture or who fitted it out. No designer, scenographer or architect is
credited in any source. For an Atlas about space, this record is strong on
programme and empty on architecture.

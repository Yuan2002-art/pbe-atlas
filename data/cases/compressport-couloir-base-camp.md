## Description
For UTMB race week 2026, Compressport used **Couloir Café at 204 Avenue Michel
Croz, Chamonix** — a third-party café — as what it calls, in its own words,
*"our Base Camp for the week"*, inviting people to "take a coffee break".

The space is not Compressport's and nothing in the retrieved sources says it was
rebuilt, rebranded or fitted out. What made it a base camp was the programme
attached to it and one thing at the door.

**The Born to Race challenge starts there.** Compressport set a timed Strava
segment of **2.92 km with 196 m of climbing**, beginning at Couloir Café, and
framed it directly against its own team: *"Think you can outperform our elite
athletes?"* Entry is by form with a screenshot of the activity from Strava,
Garmin, COROS or Suunto. The overall male and female leaders were decided on the
evening of **Sunday 30 August**. The wider challenge had opened on 3 August, so
the café was the physical door onto something already running.

Elsewhere in the same week Compressport ran a **community morning run** of up to
5 km on Wednesday 26 August, 09:00–10:30, from the Fan Experience in the
Ultra-Trail Village — a different location — with a welcome gift and a snack for
each participant, plus athlete meet-and-greets and a sales point for its current
range.

Compressport comes to this edition as the **official compression supplier of
HOKA UTMB Mont-Blanc for the thirteenth consecutive year**.

## Verification notes
**It is the second year, and the brand says so.** The record could only note
that earlier editions were thinly sourced. Compressport's own opening-day post
reads "for the second year in a row, we open our support station at Couloir
Cafe", which puts a 2025 edition behind this one on the brand's own word. It
does not settle the 2024 edition an aggregator reports, and that stays
unsourced.

**Two things were inside that the record did not have.** The brand names a
"COMPRESSPORT pic nic" served with the coffee, and a **"Born To Race Area and
segment start"** — so the challenge was not only timed from the door, it had a
dedicated area within the café. That is the first evidence of any Compressport
fit-out inside the venue, and it is the brand's claim rather than an observed
one.

**A prize the record missed:** besides the race kit for the fastest man and
woman, five entrants were drawn at random to win a Free Belt Pro 2.0.

**A photographer is credited and has not been retrieved.** The post ends
"Photos and videos @unitestudio.fr". That studio's site carries a Compressport
project, but it is a different one — "The Science of Speed", which never
mentions Couloir, Chamonix or UTMB. **Photographs of this café exist somewhere
and are the obvious next source for this record.**

**The brand's page has no year in it, and may be rewritten.** The address of
Compressport's UTMB week page carries no year, and the page dates itself only
by weekday — "Sunday 30 August" fits 2026. What is recorded here is the page as
read on 17 September 2026. A research pass reported that an earlier copy of the
same address carried a previous year's content; that could not be reproduced
from the archived HTML, so it is not asserted, but the next edition may well
overwrite this one.

**The primary source is the brand's own, and its dates are internally wrong.**
Compressport's UTMB Week page lists the week as day tabs reading "Monday 25/08"
through "Sunday 31/08", and one of them reads "Saturday 30/01" — a month typo.
In 2026, 24 August is a Monday and 30 August is a Sunday, so the weekday labels
are shifted a day late. The same page's prize text says the leaders are decided
on "Sunday, August 30th", which is correct. This record uses **24–30 August**:
it matches the edition, matches the organiser, matches the brand's own prize
date, and is what the third-party listing gives.

**Three pictures, and none shows the week as it ran.** Two are Compressport's
own page graphics and one is the cafe's own photograph of itself, undated and
showing ordinary trading rather than the base camp. **Nothing photographic
confirms any Compressport presence inside Couloir Cafe** -- no signage, no
stand, no branded fit-out -- which is consistent with the record's own reading
that the space was not rebuilt. The segment map is the strongest of the three,
because it independently confirms the 2.92 km / 196 m figures and marks the
cafe as the start.

**An image filed under the brand's 2024 folder was deliberately not used**, and
neither were press-kit images whose alt text is a word processor's
autogenerated description.

**The opening hours are not from the brand.** 08:00–17:00 daily comes from the
Marathon Weekend listing, filed as `other`. Compressport's own page gives no
hours for Couloir at all. The same listing supplies the prize as "a full race
kit" where the brand's page says only "Grand Prize" for the overall leaders.

**The address is documented but the pin is not.** Compressport gives 204 Avenue
Michel Croz; that was not geocoded here, so the coordinate is Chamonix town
centre and the precision is `city`.

**This is one of at least three Compressport locations that week**, and the
record covers only this one. The morning run was at the Ultra-Trail Village, and
the brand was also reported at the Les Contamines cheer zone. The third-party
listing splits Compressport into two entries — this café and a separate
"Compressport UTMB Week" stand — and treating the café as the case is a choice:
it is the only one of the three with a fixed address, a duration and a name the
brand gave it.

**Nothing is known about the space itself.** No source describes what
Compressport did to Couloir — signage, takeover, a corner, a counter, or nothing
at all. For a record in an atlas of spaces, the strongest facts here are a
segment and a schedule, and the weakest is the room.

**No post-event material.** Every source predates or spans the week. There is no
confirmation the café operated as described, no leaderboard result, and no
photographs.

**`primarySpatialType` is `activation`.** It is not `permanent-retail` because the
premises are someone else's, and not `pop-up` because nothing indicates a
temporary retail build. `secondarySpatialType` is empty: the field exists for
a space that genuinely takes two forms, and this is one room running one
programme. `activation` is not a compromise here, it is the accurate name.

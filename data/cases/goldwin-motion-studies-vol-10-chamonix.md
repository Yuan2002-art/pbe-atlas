## Description
**A holiday chalet turned into a test base for one material.** From 24 to 29
August 2026 — "during race week in Chamonix" — Goldwin and Product Index ran
*Motion Studies by Goldwin Vol. 10* at Japonyard, 52 Chemin du Lai in
Chamonix — "an installation and workshop series directed by Product Index,
built around Goldwin's Voxeloft technology." Japonyard is a self-catering
holiday chalet: the regional tourism listing gives two independent apartments,
six bedrooms and room for sixteen.

**Testing is the stated purpose of the closed half.** On 24 August (a hike)
and 25 August (a run), invite-only sessions took Voxeloft into the mountains.
Both "are structured around testing Voxeloft technology in real alpine
conditions, with session data and observations through the Product Index
platform."

**The open half is two runs and two mornings.** The *SAP Good Energy
Shakeout* on 26 August was "a 6K community run at social pace", with coffee
and pancakes back at the installation afterwards. *Tour du Chamonix by
Natalia* on 27 August was "an athlete led morning run through Chamonix." Both
met at 08:00 and left at 08:15 and asked for an RSVP. After each, the
installation was open to anyone from 10:00 to 12:00 to "learn more about
Voxeloft technology and the Product Index workshop", with no reservation.

**It is the tenth volume of a Goldwin series.** Motion Studies predates
Chamonix and Product Index. Volume 0, *Performance fuel*, was held at Goldwin
Marunouchi in Tokyo with Maurten Japan and PostCoffee in November 2025.
Volume 2, *An Experiment in Physical Awareness*, with GRIND magazine,
photographed "the state of the body and clothing before and after a run."
Volume 3, *Marathon Carbo*, met at Goldwin Kyoto on 8 February 2026 for a 6K
run to a soba restaurant and a carbohydrate-loading masterclass before the
Kyoto Marathon. Each volume retrieved pairs Goldwin with a different partner.

**Who Product Index is**, from its own account: a platform that scores
products on published axes. Its shoe entries rate Cushion, Response,
Stability and Transition on numbered scales, and its posts point to "the
Product Index web platform" for the full entry.

## Verification notes
**Four pictures, and all four are the building as a holiday let in 2015.**
This record still has **no image of the Motion Studies installation**, and
these do not pretend otherwise: they are the tourism board's listing
photographs of Chalet Japonyard, taken eleven years before the week this record
describes, showing furniture and fittings that have no relationship to what
Goldwin installed.

They are included under the picture rule's third category - official
photographs of the venue building - because the alternative was a page with
nothing on it at all, and because the chalet's character is itself part of what
the case is about. Every caption says the date and says it is the building.

**The only first-party material for this activation is an Instagram post**,
and what it holds has not been retrieved. That is the gap to close.

**Moved from the aggregator to the brand.** The Atlas previously recorded
this installation as resting on a single third-party listing, and it was left
unwritten for that reason. That listing links its own origin: a collaborative
Instagram post published under both Product Index's and Goldwin's global
accounts. Every fact in the description comes from that post, from Goldwin's
own posts for the earlier volumes, or from the tourism listing for the
building.

**Written from the plan, not from the week.** The post is dated 17 August,
before anything happened. Nothing retrieved confirms that the sessions ran,
how many people came, or what data was collected. Product Index's and
Goldwin's public profiles, as served to a logged-out browser on 22 September,
show only posts from September, none of them about Chamonix. This is the same
position as the Voxeloft Trail Jacket case: the record is the published
strategy.

**"Rented" is an inference, and the record does not state it.** Japonyard is
listed as a holiday rental open all year, which is why earlier notes in this
Atlas called it a rented chalet. Neither Goldwin nor Product Index says how
they came to use it.

**The listing's hours were not used.** The aggregator gives 09:00–18:00
daily, 129 hours in all. The post gives no general opening hours at all —
only the sessions and the two 10:00–12:00 open mornings. The listing also
attaches coffee and pancakes to both open runs; the post attaches them to the
26 August shakeout only. And its schedule includes the Voxeloft Trail Jacket
launch, which happened at the Ultra-Trail Village and is a separate case.

**"Natalia" is not identified.** The post gives a first name only. The
aggregator names a Natalia Mastrota at the rabbit Clubhouse the same week;
nothing connects the two, and the record does not.

**SAP's role is not established.** Its name is in the title of one run. That
is evidence of the name, not of a sponsorship.

**Volumes 1 and 4 to 9 were not found.** Three earlier volumes were
retrieved, all in Japan and all at or from Goldwin stores. Whether Vol. 10 is
the first outside Japan cannot be said from that.

**The coordinate is the tourism board's.** The listing carries a pin for 52
Chemin du Lai, which agrees with the aggregator's to within about ten metres.

**No picture is stored.** The post carries images; none was downloaded, and
none was looked at closely enough to caption. The page uses the locator.

**What is not known:** what the installation physically consisted of, what
"session data" was measured and with what, who was invited, and whether the
data became Product Index entries.

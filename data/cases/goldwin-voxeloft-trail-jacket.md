## Description
**A jacket released into a stand rather than into a shop.** For UTMB
Mont-Blanc 2026 Goldwin put the **Voxeloft Trail Jacket** out in limited
numbers at the manufacturer exhibition area of the Ultra-Trail Village on Place
du Mont-Blanc. The brand states it plainly on its own technology page, under
the heading *"Limited quantities available at Ultra-Trail® Village 2026"*: the
jacket "will be available in limited quantities at the manufacturer exhibition
area in the Ultra-Trail® Village" during an event "which draws over 10,000
runners from around the globe."

**This is the first case in the Atlas whose leading form is a product launch.**
The space is not a room the brand built and not a programme it ran; it is a
stand inside the organiser's own trade village, and the thing that makes it a
space at all is a garment available there and nowhere else.

**What the garment is.** Goldwin describes the Voxeloft Trail Jacket as "a
waterproof jacket developed by Goldwin Tech Lab" whose thermal performance is
tuned by "adjusting the amount of air inside the garment." The construction is
given precisely: "a three-layer air structure on the front and outer sleeves
and a two-layer structure on the back, while omitting air layers in the hood
and underarms, it achieves an optimal balance between warmth and mobility."
The outer fabric is PERTEX SHIELD PRO.

**The argument is made through an architectural analogy, in the brand's own
words.** Voxeloft is "Goldwin's proprietary structural design technology that
uses a layered air structure to deliver flexible thermal regulation," and
Goldwin says where the idea came from: it was "inspired by triple-pane glass
used to increase building insulation." The company calls the result "an
unprecedented hybrid outerwear piece that seamlessly combines two distinct
items into one: a lightweight rain jacket and a heavy insulation jacket."

**Where it sits relative to the event.** Goldwin's page links directly to the
organiser's Ultra-Trail Village page, which is the exhibition area brands take
stands in. Goldwin is therefore an exhibitor at the Village, not a named
partner of the race — nothing retrieved establishes any partner status.

## Verification notes
**The brand is the source for the space, the product and the scarcity.** All
three come from Goldwin's own technology page, which is why this record does
not rest on the aggregator that surfaced it. The aggregator is cited for one
thing only, below.

**The dates are the Village's, not a published opening.** Goldwin gives no
dates at all — only "Ultra-Trail® Village 2026" and "the end of August." The
listing gives 24 August as a single day and says the hours it shows are a
placeholder rather than real ones. Neither is a published run, so the record
takes the **edition's own dates, 24-30 August**, on the basis that the jacket
was available at the Village and the Village ran that week. That is an
inference about duration from a sourced fact about place, and it is the
weakest thing here.

**The exact stand is not recorded.** "The manufacturer exhibition area in the
Ultra-Trail Village" is as precise as the brand gets. The coordinate is the
Village on Place du Mont-Blanc, shared with the other Village cases in this
Atlas, and precision is `approximate` because no stand number was published.

**Written in the future tense, and left that way.** Goldwin's page says the
jacket "will be available", which means the page was written before the week
and has not been updated since. Nothing retrieved confirms the jacket was
actually on the stand, how many there were, or whether they sold. What is
recorded here is the plan the brand published, which is the thing this Atlas
studies.

**Price is not recorded** — Goldwin's page shows "Coming Soon" against the
online store rather than a figure, and no price was published for the Village
release.

**Not the same as Motion Studies Vol. 10.** Goldwin also had an installation
at Japonyard, a holiday chalet on Chemin du Lai, that week, directed by Product
Index. That is a different space at a different address with a different
mechanism, and it is its own case. Keeping them apart matters — one is a
stand in the organiser's village, the other a chalet the brand took over.

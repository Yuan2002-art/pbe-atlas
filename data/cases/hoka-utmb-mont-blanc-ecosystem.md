## Description
HOKA holds the Title Partner position for the UTMB World Series' flagship events
through 2028, announced in April 2024, having entered as Premier Technical
Footwear and Apparel Partner in October 2021. The race is formally titled
**HOKA UTMB Mont-Blanc**.

For the 2026 edition (24–30 August) the brand operated across three sites rather
than one. **HOKA Base Camp**, on the terrace of La Folie Douce Hotels, appears in
the organiser's own animation programme, hosting the HOKA UTMB Mont-Blanc
Shakeout Run on Tuesday 25 August and a celebration for athlete Ludovic Pommeret
on Wednesday 26 August; trade coverage adds that it ran for ten days and
contained an RMC broadcast set and a finisher area with recovery, massage and
personalisation of an exclusive finisher jacket. **HOKA FlyLab**, on Place du
Mont-Blanc inside the Ultra-Trail Village, showcased the Tecton X 4 with a free
24-hour shoe trial and a 24-hour radio station, "On Top Of The World". **HOKA
Refuge**, at the La Flégère mountain station reached by a HOKA-branded cable car
run with Compagnie du Mont-Blanc, offered hiking and trail product testing with
access to trails on the race route.

## Verification notes
**Evidence is strong at the centre and thinner at the edges.** The partnership,
the event's official title and the existence and programming of Base Camp all
come from the organiser. FlyLab and Refuge do **not** appear on the organiser's
animation page — they rest on two Italian trade outlets that appear to derive
from a single HOKA press release, so they are not independent of each other.

**One unresolved discrepancy:** the organiser lists the shakeout run at 08:30,
the Italian trade press at 09:00.

**The pin is one of three sites.** Base Camp is used because it is the
organiser-confirmed location; FlyLab (Place du Mont-Blanc) and Refuge
(La Flégère, above the valley) are not separately plotted.

**No agency is credited for the 2026 build.** For the 2023 edition — a different
year, recorded here only as context — the agency **Exhibit Group** credits itself
with a four-site build (Alpina Eclectic Hotel, Brasserie de l'M, a media chalet
and La Folie Douce) with Pixeolab on AV. That is a self-reported portfolio page.

**A claim deliberately excluded:** a Substack newsletter describes FlyLab as
two-storey. No official or trade source corroborates this, so it is not recorded.

## Strategic purpose
Make the brand inseparable from the event. Not presence at UTMB but occupation of
it — the name on the race, the shoe on the start line, and physical territory at
the three points where the week actually happens: the expo, the mountain and the
finish.

## Archive use
The pilot's maximal case, and the benchmark the other four are measured against.
Demonstrates what total sponsorship looks like spatially, which is what makes
ASICS' unaffiliated pop-up legible as a strategy rather than a smaller budget.

## Why this location?
Three locations, each doing a different job. The expo captures volume. The
mountain station puts the product on the terrain it is sold for, which no
show-floor booth can do. The hotel terrace holds the emotional end of the week —
the finish, the recovery, the athlete tribute. Together they cover the whole arc
of a participant's stay rather than one moment in it.

## Why this timing?
Ten days for Base Camp against seven for the race. The overhang is the point:
the brand is in place before the audience arrives and still there after the
finish, which is when the emotional payload of an ultra actually lands.

## Why this event?
UTMB is the sport's centre of gravity, and the title position is exclusive —
buying it locks the category's most valuable association away from every
competitor for the duration of the deal. The 2028 horizon turns an activation
into an occupation.

## Why this product?
Tecton X 4 is a carbon-plated racing shoe, so it needs the most credible possible
context. A 24-hour free trial at the event where it will be raced is a claim that
the product survives contact with the actual conditions — a claim a retail wall
cannot make.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
That HOKA equipment is what this race is run in. The claim is settled not by
argument but by saturation: the event carries the name, the athletes carry the
shoes, and the recovery area catches the finishers.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Institutional belonging rather than subcultural credibility. HOKA is positioned
as infrastructure of the sport — a host, not a visitor. The Pommeret tribute
buys lineage the brand's own short history cannot supply.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Load-bearing at the Refuge, replaceable elsewhere. Testing shoes on the actual
race terrain cannot be simulated. The radio station, the broadcast set and the
finisher area are media production as much as visitor experience — they are
built to be transmitted, and most of their audience is not in Chamonix.

## Experience mechanism
Envelopment. The visitor does not choose to enter a HOKA space; they move through
a week in which the expo, the mountain lift and the finish are all already
branded. Consent is structural rather than given at a door.

## Key strategic insight
Title sponsorship converts spatial strategy from persuasion into infrastructure:
once the brand owns the event's name, its physical sites no longer have to argue
for attention, and can instead be distributed across the full arc of a
participant's week — expo, mountain, finish.

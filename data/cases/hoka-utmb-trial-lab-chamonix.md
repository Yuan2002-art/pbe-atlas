## Description
A five-day pop-up in a vacant retail unit on the main street, fitted with a short
indoor test ramp of varied gradients and surfaces, a loan desk issuing shoes for
morning trail runs, and a single rack of a colourway sold only in the valley.

## Strategic purpose
Convert scepticism into sensation. Because the product's central idea is
physically obvious within a few steps, the fastest route to belief is to get the
shoe onto the visitor's foot.

## Archive use
Cleanest example in the Atlas of product trial as the primary mechanism, with
retail and archive functions deliberately stripped away.

## Why this location?
A shopfront on the pedestrian main street intercepts casual foot traffic, unlike
an expo stand that requires a decision to attend.

## Why this timing?
Race week concentrates an audience that is unusually willing to change equipment
because it is actively thinking about its feet.

## Why this event?
The event provides both the audience and the terrain: a loan shoe can be tested
on genuine alpine trail within minutes of leaving the door, which no city pop-up
can offer.

## Why this product?
A valley-exclusive colourway gives the visit a transactional reason as well as an
experiential one, and quietly measures how many trial participants convert.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
That maximal cushioning is an advantage in long mountain descents rather than a
compromise — a claim that is contested, and therefore must be felt to be
accepted.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Openness, bordering on informality: no gatekeeping, no athlete hierarchy, anyone
may borrow a pair. It positions the brand against the specialist seriousness of
its neighbours in the same street.

## What role does the physical experience play?
It is the entire argument. Without a foot in the shoe, the case collapses into a
claim printed on a wall.

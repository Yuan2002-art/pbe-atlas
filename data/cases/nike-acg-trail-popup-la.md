## Description
A month-long pop-up in a converted warehouse presenting the ACG line through
local geography: a wall-sized map of Los Angeles trailheads, archival ACG
garments from the 1990s, weekend shuttle runs to the Angeles National Forest, and
a graphic release sold only at this address.

## Strategic purpose
Give a mass brand's outdoor sub-line a specific place to belong, borrowing the
credibility of a local trail community it does not own.

## Archive use
Example of scale borrowing specificity: used to ask whether a large brand can
acquire subcultural authority by renting proximity to it.

## Why this location?
The Arts District supplies design-literate foot traffic, while sitting close
enough to the San Gabriel foothills that the shuttle runs are plausible rather
than symbolic.

## Why this timing?
Early spring, when the Southern California trail season is at its most pleasant
and the audience is choosing equipment for the year.

## Why this event?
Not attached to a race. The absence is deliberate: the case tests whether a brand
can generate an occasion rather than borrow one.

## Why this product?
Reissued 1990s ACG garments carry the line's own history, which is the one asset
a competitor cannot replicate — and the newly released graphic converts that
history into present-tense scarcity.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
A comparatively weak one. The claim is lineage — that the brand was equipping
this terrain decades ago — rather than any measurable current advantage.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Local belonging, assembled quickly: the map, the collective partnership and the
city-exclusive graphic all argue that the brand is *from here*.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Largely staging. Most of the argument is visual and could be delivered as a
campaign; the shuttle runs are the only element that the visitor's body is
required for.

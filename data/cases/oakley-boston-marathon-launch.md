## Description
A four-day launch space near the finish line presenting a racing eyewear model
through measurement rather than styling: lens samples under different light
sources, a weight-comparison bench, fit sessions with optical staff, and a
signature edition released on race morning.

## Strategic purpose
Make a small, easily overlooked object argue for itself at the one moment when
runners are paying attention to marginal equipment gains.

## Archive use
Used to examine a scale problem: how a brand builds spatial experience around a
product that is almost invisible on the body.

## Why this location?
The finish-line block is the highest-attention retail address of the weekend, and
places the product where its athletes will be photographed wearing it hours
later.

## Why this timing?
The days before the race, when the field is assembled, has time to spare, and is
in an equipment-optimising frame of mind.

## Why this event?
A qualifying-standard field is an unusually performance-literate audience,
willing to accept an argument made in grams and light transmission figures.

## Why this product?
Racing eyewear is bought on fit and weight, both of which require handling — so
the product's purchase logic already demands a physical space.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
That measurable marginal advantage exists in a category usually sold on
appearance, and that the collaborating athlete accepted it under competition.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Seriousness as identity. The refusal of styling language positions the buyer as
someone who races rather than someone who looks the part.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Decisive at the point of sale: weight and fit cannot be transmitted through a
screen, so the bench and the fit session do work no amount of description can.

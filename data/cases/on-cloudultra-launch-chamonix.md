## Description
A three-day launch room presenting one shoe as a research result: cut-away
midsole sections, force-plate data printed at wall scale, a short instrumented
treadmill session open to visitors, and fifty numbered pairs released on the
final evening.

## Strategic purpose
Argue from data in a valley where every competitor argues from terrain, and so
occupy a position nobody else in the street is claiming.

## Archive use
Used to contrast laboratory credibility with mountain credibility: two different
proofs offered to the same audience in the same week, 400 metres apart.

## Why this location?
A first-floor room rather than a shopfront filters the audience to people willing
to climb a flight of stairs, which suits a launch that rewards attention over
footfall.

## Why this timing?
Launching during the event borrows its audience while the product category is at
maximum salience, and dates the shoe to a specific competitive moment.

## Why this event?
UTMB supplies an audience fluent enough to read force-plate data as meaningful
rather than decorative — a rare condition for a technical launch.

## Why this product?
A long-distance shoe is the hardest product in the range to evaluate in a shop,
so substituting measurement for intuition is both a necessity and a positioning
choice.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
That the advantage is engineered and quantified, developed with an external
research partner rather than asserted by the brand alone.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Precision as an aesthetic. The room reads as a laboratory, aligning the brand
with scientific neutrality rather than with mountain subculture.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Substantial but not indispensable: the data could be published online, yet the
cut-away objects and the instrumented treadmill convert an abstract claim into
something the visitor has personally verified.

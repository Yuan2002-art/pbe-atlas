## Description
A temporary timber-and-fabric structure in the centre of Chamonix for the race
week, housing athlete kit preparation in full public view, a pole and footwear
service bench, and a wall of race-worn prototypes labelled with the races they
completed.

## Strategic purpose
Position the brand as the working infrastructure of the race rather than one of
its advertisers, by making the least glamorous activity — preparation and repair —
the most visible.

## Archive use
Primary example of the performance archive assembled *live*: objects gain their
archival status during the week rather than being brought in already historic.

## Why this location?
The central square is the one point every participant, crew member and spectator
passes repeatedly, which converts a single structure into many exposures without
additional sites.

## Why this timing?
Race week compresses the sport's entire audience into one valley for seven days,
so a structure that would be uneconomic anywhere else becomes efficient.

## Why this event?
The event supplies something the brand cannot manufacture: unscripted evidence.
Kit that finishes the race becomes proof, and kit that fails does so in public,
which is the risk that makes the claim credible.

## Why this product?
Prototype race kit is not for sale, which removes the transactional reading and
lets the structure argue about development capability instead of assortment.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
That the equipment is developed with and by competitors at the limit of the
sport, and that the brand is present at the moment of failure as well as
the moment of victory.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Craft and service. The mechanic's bench is a deliberately humble image inside a
sport increasingly filled with polished brand environments.

## What role does the physical experience play?
It supplies witness. The persuasive content is watching a technician work on a
real athlete's kit hours before a start — an experience with no digital
equivalent.

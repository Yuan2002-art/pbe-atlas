## Description
SATISFY operated an "Off-Road Supply & Fuel Station" at 133 Chemin du Nant Favre
in Chamonix during UTMB week, open 24–29 August, 10:00–18:00. The brand's own
page describes it as the third year it has run the format. Inside: retail with
products released in-store first, a fuel station, the chance to meet SATISFY pro
athletes, and community runs departing from the door every morning — one of
which was separately ticketed as "Off-Road Supply Chamonix Run #1", Monday
24 August, 08:30.

Products named on the page include a SATISFY × Oakley Suture Jacket, a SATISFY
COROS APEX 4 watch, a Justice Cordura hydration vest, a Hydrapak 500 ml soft
flask, MothTech T-shirts and TheROCKER shoe.

## Verification notes
**The edition year is inferred, not stated.** The activation copy carries no
year. 2026 is triangulated from the Eventbrite listing (Monday 24 August — the
24th falls on a Monday in 2026, not 2025) and from UTMB Mont-Blanc's own 2026
dates of 24–30 August.

**Coordinates are street-level, not the building.** Nominatim resolved Chemin du
Nant Favre but not house number 133, so the pin is on the street, not the door.

**No agency, studio or production partner is credited anywhere** — for any
edition. The field is absent rather than guessed.

**"Suture Jacket" is eyewear, and the name invites the wrong reading.**
"Jacket" is Oakley's own term for a frame, not a garment. The page describes
the item as having "original perforations" in a "Grenoble Green colorway", the
file SATISFY serves it under is named `prism-oakley-satisfy-glasses-grenoble-
green_front`, and the picture shows sunglasses. The `product` field keeps the
full product name because that is what it is called, but anyone reading this
record should not count a jacket among the exclusives.

**Nine pictures, and none shows the shop.** All are from SATISFY's own
activation page. Three are page imagery — a chrome-rendered motorcycle used as
the header, a figure against the massif, and a pair of frames including a group
run — and none of them is dated or located by the page. Six are product shots
of the exclusives on plain grounds. **No photograph of 133 Chemin du Nant
Favre, inside or out, was found anywhere.** For a record at `verified`, that
gap is worth stating plainly: the evidence for the space is textual.

**The header image is a made image.** It was checked rather than assumed — the
earlier concern was that the page hero might be a photograph from a previous
edition. It is not a photograph at all.

**Earlier editions are thinly sourced.** A 2024 edition (26–31 August) appears
only on a third-party event aggregator, and no direct source for 2025 was found,
so neither is recorded here. The "third year" line implies 2024–2026 but that is
inference.

## Strategic purpose
Hold a defined audience for six days without competing for attention inside the
official expo — by supplying something the expo does not: a daily reason to come
back, and product that cannot be bought anywhere else that week.

## Archive use
The pilot's clearest case of **community as the primary mechanism**, with retail
attached rather than the other way round. Pairs against HOKA as the sponsorship
counter-example.

## Why this location?
The address is not in the centre. Chemin du Nant Favre sits roughly a kilometre
and a half south-west of Place du Mont-Blanc, where the official Ultra-Trail
Village is staged. That distance is the decision: a visitor has to choose to walk
there, which filters for the brand's own audience instead of the general race
crowd, and it puts the door closer to the trail than to the expo.

## Why this timing?
UTMB week is the one period when SATISFY's dispersed international audience is
physically concentrated in one valley. A six-day lease is only economic under
that condition — and the morning-run programme only works when the guests are
already in town and already running.

## Why this event?
Less about the race than about the gathering around it. SATISFY's relationship
to competition is oblique — the brand sells running as a state rather than a
result — so it borrows the audience UTMB assembles without buying any official
association with the racing.

## Why this product?
The in-store-first release and the collaboration items — Oakley, COROS, Hydrapak
— convert a visit into a purchase window that closes. Scarcity is doing the work
that a sponsorship budget does for other brands.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
A weak one, deliberately. Nothing on site tests, measures or proves anything.
The claim is proximity: these are the people who actually run here, this is what
they carry. Authority by association with practice rather than by data.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Membership in a scene that defines itself against mainstream running culture. The
out-of-centre address, the fuel station framing and the cow bell are all signals
legible to people already inside the subculture and slightly opaque to everyone
else — which is the point.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Constitutive for the community runs, decorative for the retail. The shop could
be an online drop; the 07:00 departure from a specific door with specific people
cannot. The space exists mainly to give the run a start line.

## Experience mechanism
Daily repetition. A visitor who comes once buys; a visitor who runs every morning
joins. The programme is designed to convert a six-day window into five or six
separate visits, which is a fundamentally different mechanic from a single
impressive encounter.

## Key strategic insight
SATISFY buys the audience UTMB assembles without buying UTMB — substituting a
recurring daily ritual and exclusive product for official status, and accepting a
smaller, self-selecting crowd as the price of not competing inside the expo.

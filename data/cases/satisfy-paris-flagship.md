## Description
A small permanent store combining apparel retail with a listening room of records
and cassettes, a wall of tour-style printed matter, and a Thursday evening group
run that departs from the door and returns for drinks.

## Strategic purpose
Make running legible as a subculture rather than a fitness activity, so that
membership — not performance improvement — becomes the reason to buy.

## Archive use
The key counter-case for the classification system: a space whose archive is
entirely cultural, with no performance evidence displayed at all.

## Why this location?
The Marais places the store inside fashion and record-shop adjacency rather than
sports retail, which frames the product as clothing with a position rather than
equipment with a specification.

## Why this timing?
Opened as the brand moved from wholesale distribution to direct sales, at the
point where controlling the surrounding atmosphere became commercially necessary.

## Why this event?
Not event-dependent. Its rhythm is weekly rather than annual, which makes it a
useful control against the event-led cases in this Atlas.

## Why this product?
Cassettes and printed matter cost little and signal much: they make the taste
argument explicit and give regulars a reason to return between apparel seasons.

## What performance claim is being reinforced?
Almost none in the conventional sense. The claim is about the *experience* of
running long — the trance state — which is subjective and therefore cannot be
disproved.

## What cultural meaning is being built?
Belonging to a specific, deliberately narrow scene. The store operates as a
filter: legible to those already inside it, opaque to a general audience.

## What role does the physical experience play?
Central. The atmosphere — light, volume, music, the people present — is the
product's actual context, and it is the part that cannot be reproduced on a
website.

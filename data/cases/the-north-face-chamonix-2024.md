## Description
**A UTMB week without the title.** Ahead of UTMB 2024 a Chamonix blog
noted that The North Face was "Not an official sponsor of UTMB anymore, but…
is gearing up and promoting a 'big week' in Chamonix". The brand's answer, as
its agency Chaiten describes it, was to "Place The North Face shop in Chamonix
at the core of the experience".

**One colour for a week.** The campaign was *The Point Of Difference*, "in
collaboration with artist Yves Klein: The IKB (International Klein Blue)
Collection", to "showcase the Summit Series products and the IKB line". The
agency's stated aim: "to paint Chamonix blue with an immersive activation
program and impactful stunts throughout the week."

**What happened in the shop, by purpose.** The agency's plan sorts it into
four:

- "to drive traffic: DJ sets, live race broadcasts, workshops."
- "to boost conversions: live customizations with artist Ombeline."
- "to engage the community: breakfast with athletes / runs with athletes,
  always-on product testing."
- "to create emotional engagement through Yves Klein's unique universe: The
  Blue Run Party."

**A second agency, and the first photographs.** Alongside the Chamonix agency
that wrote the campaign, the German event agency **Rheinsport** ran the
physical delivery, and publishes the UTMB 2024 week as its own project: client
The North Face, location France, date 2024, service Event. It describes a booth
where "the latest trail shoes" could be tested "at the booth and on the trails",
"treadmill analyses", live product launches, and group runs, plus athlete
meetings with Pau Capell and Ida-Sophie Hegemann, giveaways of Transgrancanaria
race entries, and discounts from local partners. Its photographs show a
street-facing **Performance Lab** booth built around a treadmill, a
membership-programme gazebo, a shoe-test tent racked floor to ceiling, and a
group assembling at the shop door.

**The week was one of thirty-six.** Rheinsport states that UTMB was "just one
highlight of a total of 36 trail events across six countries" it ran for the
brand that year, and that the two have worked together "over 13 years". This
record is one stop on a touring programme, not a one-off.

## Verification notes
**Nine photographs, from the delivery agency, and the year is stated.** The
page prints "Date: 2024" and "Location: France" and files every image under
`UTMB2024_`. No photographer is credited, and **no individual frame is
captioned** by the agency. That matters, because the same page says the agency
ran 36 trail events in six countries that season: the filenames and the project
page place these at UTMB, but nothing rules out a frame having strayed in from
another stop. The captions describe what is visible and claim nothing more.

**The blue is consistent with the campaign but is not asserted to be it.** The
other agency describes a plan to "paint Chamonix blue" around Yves Klein's IKB,
and these photographs are heavily blue — kit, backdrops, the treadmill deck.
The two accounts have not been connected by any source, so the record notes the
consistency without treating it as evidence.

**Two agencies are now credited for one week**, which was not previously known:
a Chamonix agency for brief, strategy and programme, and a German agency for
event delivery. Neither page mentions the other.

**Chaiten's own gallery is still gone.** The 22 files its archived page lists
are not held by the Wayback Machine and the site remains down. That dead end is
unchanged; these photographs come from elsewhere.

**An agency's words, not the brand's.** The only detailed source is Chaiten's
case study, which states the brief and its own response. No North Face page
for the week was read. The blog confirms the programme's outline — athlete
breakfasts, group runs, DJs — before the week.

**Read from an archive.** Chaiten's live site refused connections; the record
uses a Wayback capture of 19 February 2026. The page's own title is "UTMB

**Which shop, and where.** The agency says "The North Face shop in Chamonix"
